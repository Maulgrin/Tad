# WELCOME TO BUG WARS

BUG Wars is a military science-fiction roleplaying game about ordinary soldiers facing extraordinary numbers.

You do not play superheroes.

You play riflemen, medics, scouts, engineers, gunners, and squad leaders dropped into places where the situation is already going wrong.

Your squad may be ordered to:

- hold a relay station against a mass assault;
- clear tunnels beneath a mining colony;
- recover survivors from a dead settlement;
- mark a biological artillery organism for orbital fire;
- destroy a nest complex;
- escort engineers through an active combat zone;
- recover intelligence before the Bugs overrun the site;
- survive long enough for extraction.

The game focuses on **small-unit decisions inside very large battles**.

The soldiers beside you matter. Your ammunition matters. The order to hold matters. And when the wall comes down, what you do next matters most.

# THE WAR

> **FIELD TRANSMISSION // 18TH EXPEDITIONARY**
>
> “Stop counting them.
>
> You count ammunition. You count breathing people. You count minutes until evac.
>
> You do not count Bugs.”
>
> — Sgt. Mara Venn, Kestral Ridge

Humanity's expansion beyond its home system created hundreds of frontier settlements before anyone understood how widespread the subterranean ecosystems were.

The first encounters were dismissed as isolated animal attacks.

Then entire mining crews disappeared.

Then colonies stopped transmitting.

Then survey teams discovered tunnel networks kilometers deep.

The creatures became known simply as **Bugs**.

That name hides a dangerous truth: the enemy is not one species. It is an ecology built for war.

Warriors attack in waves. Winged castes cross walls. Burrowers appear inside defensive lines. Heavy organisms smash fortified positions. Artillery organisms launch biological plasma across extraordinary distances. Command organisms coordinate attacks that should be impossible for animals.

Human militaries adapted.

The result is the **Expeditionary Infantry**: mobile troops trained to deploy quickly, survive hostile environments, and fight enemies whose numbers can turn any defensive line into a temporary position.

The war is not a single front. It is a chain of colonies, worlds, stations, moons, and forgotten outposts connected by casualty reports.

# WHAT MAKES BUG WARS DIFFERENT?

### Squad Warfare
Every character has a battlefield role, but no one fights alone.

### Ammunition Pressure
Automatic weapons are powerful. The question is not whether you can fire more. The question is whether you can afford to.

### Suppression
A soldier can be combat ineffective without being physically wounded. Explosions, casualties, charging swarms, and collapsing positions all affect morale.

### Command
Leadership has rules. A capable squad leader can rally troops, coordinate fire, organize retreats, and keep units fighting.

### Hordes
Fifty Bugs do not require fifty initiative entries. BUG Wars treats masses of creatures as battlefield entities.

### Mission Play
The goal is rarely “kill everything.” The goal is to finish the job and get out.

# CHAPTER 1
# THE INFANTRY

> **“Everybody wants orbital support. Nobody remembers somebody still has to stand on the ground and tell orbit where to shoot.”**

A BUG Wars character is a trained infantry trooper assigned to an expeditionary combat unit. The character rules are designed around competence rather than destiny. Your trooper already knows how to fight. Advancement represents experience, specialization, leadership, and survival.

## Creating a Trooper

Character creation follows eight steps.

### 1. Define the Soldier
Choose a Name, Call Sign, Homeworld or Colony, Service Background, Personality, and Reason for Enlisting.

Consider one sentence that answers:

> **Why are you still here?**

### 2. Choose an Archetype

| Archetype | Battlefield Role |
|---|---|
| Rifleman | Flexible line infantry |
| Squad Leader | Command and coordination |
| Heavy Weapons Specialist | Volume fire and anti-armor |
| Combat Medic | Casualty survival |
| Scout | Reconnaissance and movement |
| Combat Engineer | Demolitions, repair, fortification |

Archetypes are not restrictive classes. Any trooper can shoot, run, fight, carry wounded personnel, or attempt first aid. The archetype identifies what the character is especially good at when the situation becomes difficult.

### 3. Assign Attributes
Assign **4, 3, 2, 2** among Strength, Agility, Wits, and Empathy.

### 4. Assign Skills
Choose one Skill at **3**, two Skills at **2**, three Skills at **1**, and leave the rest at **0**. At least one Archetype Key Skill must be 3, and a second must be at least 2.

### 5. Choose a Specialty
Select one starting Specialty from your Archetype.

### 6. Calculate Derived Values

**Health:** 3 + Strength  
**Resolve:** 2 + Empathy  
**Carry:** 4 + Strength

### 7. Choose Rank
Most starting troopers are Privates. Squad Leaders may begin as Corporals. Veteran campaigns may begin at higher rank.

### 8. Take Your Loadout
A standard combat loadout includes Standard Combat Armor, a Primary Weapon, Service Pistol, four Primary Weapon Reloads, two Pistol Reloads, two Fragmentation Grenades, Tactical Radio, and Basic Field Kit.

# CHAPTER 2
# THE RULES OF WAR

> **FIELD MANUAL 3-7:** “Simple rules survive contact. Complicated rules get soldiers killed.”

BUG Wars uses **step dice**.

## 2.1 Ratings and Dice

Attributes and trained Skills use the following ladder:

| Rating | Die |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No die |
| 1 | d6 |
| 2 | d8 |
| 3 | d10 |
| 4 | d12 |

Attributes normally range from 1 to 4.

Skills range from 0 to 4.

A normal test rolls:

> **Attribute Die + Skill Die**

If the character has Skill 0, roll only the Attribute Die.

### Example

A trooper with **Agility 3** and **Ranged Combat 2** rolls:

> **d10 + d8**

## 2.2 Counting Successes

Each die can generate successes.

| Die Result | Successes |
|---:|---:|
| 1-5 | 0 |
| 6-9 | 1 |
| 10-11 | 2 |
| 12 | 3 |

A d6 can therefore produce at most 1 success.

A d10 can produce up to 2.

A d12 can produce up to 3.

Add the successes from every die rolled.

## 2.3 Difficulty

Most dangerous actions require **1 success**.

| Difficulty | Required Successes |
|---|---:|
| Routine under pressure | 1 |
| Difficult | 2 |
| Severe | 3 |
| Extreme | 4+ |

Do not roll for ordinary actions that have no meaningful pressure or consequence.

## 2.4 Opposed Tests

When two characters directly oppose one another, both roll.

The side with more successes wins.

A tie favors:

1. the defender;
2. the character maintaining the current situation;
3. the GM if neither applies.

## 2.5 Bonus Dice

Some advantages add **Bonus Dice**.

A Bonus Die is a **d6**.

Each Bonus Die that rolls a 6 adds 1 success.

Unless a rule specifically says otherwise, a test cannot gain more than **3 Bonus Dice**.

Sources include:

- aiming;
- specialties;
- command actions;
- favorable positioning;
- Full Auto;
- assistance.

## 2.6 Step Modifiers

Some conditions step a die up or down.

A positive step changes:

> d6 -> d8 -> d10 -> d12

A negative step changes:

> d12 -> d10 -> d8 -> d6 -> no die

Apply step modifiers to the **Skill Die first**.

If there is no Skill Die, apply them to the Attribute Die.

If a die is already d12 and receives another positive step, add one Bonus Die instead.

If a Skill Die is stepped below d6, remove it. Further penalties step down the Attribute Die.

No Attribute Die can be reduced below d6.

## 2.7 Helping

One character may help another when it makes sense.

Each effective helper adds **1 Bonus Die**, to a maximum of 2 helpers.

Helping normally requires the helper to spend the same kind of action as the acting character.

## 2.8 Pushing a Roll

A character may **Push** a failed or disappointing test once.

When pushing:

1. Keep dice that already scored successes.
2. Reroll dice that scored no successes.
3. Accept the new result.
4. Gain **1 Suppression** after the roll.
5. If the action used ammunition, make an additional Ammo Check.

A roll cannot normally be pushed more than once.

The GM may prohibit pushing when there is no reasonable way for greater effort to change the result.

## 2.9 Extended Tests

Some tasks require accumulated successes over time.

Examples:

- repairing a damaged transmitter;
- cutting through a blast door;
- treating several casualties;
- planting charges along a defensive line.

The GM sets a total:

| Task | Required Total |
|---|---:|
| Short | 3 successes |
| Significant | 5 successes |
| Major | 8 successes |
| Extreme | 10+ successes |

Each roll consumes time and may trigger complications.

# CHAPTER 5
# BATTLEFIELD ROLES

# 5.1 Rifleman

The Rifleman is the flexible core of an infantry squad.

**Key Skills**

- Ranged Combat
- Athletics
- Mobility

### Controlled Burst

When using Burst fire, the weapon depletes ammunition only on an Ammo Die result of **1** instead of 1-2.

### Bug Hunter

Gain **1 Bonus Die** when attacking a Bug type the character has previously fought or studied, at Close or Medium range.

Choose known Bug types with GM approval.

### Bayonet Training

A rifle fitted with a bayonet counts as a proper melee weapon.

Gain **1 Bonus Die** on the first Close Combat attack made immediately after an enemy enters Engaged range.

---

# 5.2 Squad Leader

The Squad Leader keeps the unit functioning under pressure.

**Key Skills**

- Command
- Ranged Combat
- Recon

### Rally

Once per round, Rally may be performed as a **Fast Action** rather than a Slow Action.

### Coordinated Fire

When the Squad Leader succeeds at **Focus Fire**, one additional ally may benefit.

### Hold the Line

Allies in the same Zone gain **1 Bonus Die** on Panic Tests while the Squad Leader is conscious, able to communicate, and not Pinned.

---

# 5.3 Heavy Weapons Specialist

The Heavy Weapons Specialist controls lanes, kills large organisms, and breaks swarms.

**Key Skills**

- Heavy Weapons
- Athletics
- Demolitions

### Walking Fire

The character may move one Zone and use Suppressive Fire in the same turn without the normal movement penalty.

### Brace

Ignore the first penalty caused by Heavy weapons, unstable firing positions, or recoil.

### Explosives Expert

Gain **1 Bonus Die** on Demolitions tests involving:

- mines;
- prepared charges;
- grenades;
- breaching explosives.

---

# 5.4 Combat Medic

The Combat Medic keeps casualties alive long enough to extract them.

**Key Skills**

- Medical
- Ranged Combat
- Mobility

### Combat Triage

Stabilization may be attempted as a **Fast Action** once per round.

### Get Him Moving

After successfully stabilizing a casualty, remove **1 Suppression** from that character.

### Field Surgeon

Gain **1 Bonus Die** when treating Critical Injuries during a secure rest period.

---

# 5.5 Scout

The Scout finds the enemy before the enemy finds the squad.

**Key Skills**

- Recon
- Mobility
- Survival

### Stay Low

Gain **1 Bonus Die** on Mobility tests made to cross exposed terrain or reach cover.

### Forward Observer

Gain **1 Bonus Die** when:

- calling indirect fire;
- identifying artillery coordinates;
- directing remote weapons;
- marking a target for support.

### Ambush

When beginning combat undetected, gain **1 Bonus Die** on the first attack or Recon action of the encounter.

---

# 5.6 Combat Engineer

The Combat Engineer builds, destroys, repairs, and opens routes.

**Key Skills**

- Demolitions
- Tech
- Heavy Weapons

### Breacher

Prepared charges deal **+1 Damage** against:

- structures;
- fortifications;
- armored doors;
- vehicles;
- heavily armored Bugs.

### Field Repairs

The Engineer may attempt field repairs without a workshop if spare parts are available.

### Defensive Works

Given at least 10 minutes and suitable materials, improve one Zone's Cover by one level.

The improvement lasts until destroyed or abandoned.

# CHAPTER 6
# GEAR UP

## 6.1 Bulk

Equipment uses abstract **Bulk**.

A character can carry Bulk up to their **Carry** value without penalty.

### Encumbered

If total Bulk exceeds Carry:

- step Athletics down once;
- step Mobility down once;
- the character cannot Sprint.

If Bulk exceeds Carry by more than 2, the character cannot move more than one Zone per round without dropping equipment.

## 6.2 Ammunition Reloads

A reload is a magazine, belt, shell carrier, battery, or equivalent ammunition package.

Two standard reloads normally equal:

> **Bulk 1**

Heavy ammunition may count differently.

## 6.3 General Equipment

| Item | Bulk | Effect |
|---|---:|---|
| Tactical Radio | 0 | Secure squad communication |
| Medkit | 1 | Required for advanced Medical treatment |
| Demolition Kit | 1 | Required for prepared explosives |
| Motion Scanner | 1 | Bonus Die on some Recon tests |
| Binocular Optics | 0 | Long-range observation |
| Field Repair Kit | 1 | Required for significant Tech repairs |
| Entrenching Kit | 1 | Build or improve field cover |
| Portable Sensor | 1 | Monitor one Zone or approach |
| Ammunition Pack | 1 | Two extra standard reloads |
| Flare Pack | 0 | Illumination or signaling |

## 6.4 Grenades

A fragmentation grenade is a thrown **Blast** weapon.

Use:

> **Agility + Athletics**

Range: Close or Medium.

Damage: 3.

A character normally carries grenades as discrete items.

# CHAPTER 7
# CONTACT

Combat is divided into rounds.

A round represents several seconds of intense action.

## 7.1 Initiative

At the start of combat, each participant rolls:

> **Agility + Recon**

Count successes.

Highest success total acts first.

Ties are broken by:

1. highest individual die result;
2. larger Agility die;
3. player choice for tied PCs;
4. GM choice for tied NPCs.

Initiative normally remains fixed for the encounter.

## 7.2 Actions

Each character receives:

- **1 Slow Action**
- **1 Fast Action**

A Slow Action may be exchanged for another Fast Action.

## 7.3 Common Slow Actions

- Fire a weapon
- Full Auto
- Suppressive Fire
- Close Combat attack
- Sprint
- Treat a casualty
- Use Demolitions
- Perform a major Tech action
- Focus Fire
- Hold Position
- Fire at Will

## 7.4 Common Fast Actions

- Move one Zone
- Aim
- Take Cover
- Reload a standard weapon
- Draw or stow equipment
- Stand from Prone
- Rally
- Interact with a simple object

## 7.5 Zones

BUG Wars uses **Zones**.

A Zone is a meaningful tactical area such as:

- command bunker;
- western barricade;
- killing field;
- rocky rise;
- tunnel chamber;
- dropship pad.

A character normally moves one adjacent Zone with a Fast Action.

### Sprint

Spend a Slow Action to move up to two Zones.

Difficult terrain may require Athletics or Mobility.

## 7.6 Range Bands

| Range | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Engaged | close enough for melee |
| Close | same Zone |
| Medium | adjacent Zone |
| Long | two Zones away |
| Extreme | three or more Zones away |

A weapon cannot normally attack beyond its listed Range.

## 7.7 Cover

| Cover | Effect |
|---|---|
| Light | Step attack Skill Die down once |
| Solid | Step attack Skill Die down once; Armor +1 |
| Fortified | Step attack Skill Die down once; Armor +2 |

Examples:

**Light**
- brush;
- scattered debris;
- shallow trench.

**Solid**
- stone wall;
- vehicle engine block;
- reinforced barricade.

**Fortified**
- prepared bunker;
- heavy firing position;
- hardened defensive wall.

## 7.8 Aim

Spend a Fast Action.

Gain **1 Bonus Die** on the next Single or Burst attack made before the end of the current turn.

An Accurate weapon may improve this benefit.

## 7.9 Prone

Going Prone is a Fast Action.

While Prone:

- ranged attacks from Medium or farther step the attack Skill Die down once unless the attacker has elevation;
- melee attacks against the Prone character gain 1 Bonus Die;
- standing is a Fast Action.

## 7.10 Dodge and Block

A character may react to an attack if aware of it.

Spend a Fast Action to:

**Dodge**
> Agility + Mobility

or

**Block**
> Strength + Close Combat

Each success cancels one attacker success.

If the character has already spent their Fast Action this round, the reaction consumes the Fast Action from their next turn.

A character may make only one Dodge or Block reaction per round unless a Specialty says otherwise.

## 7.11 Ranged Attacks

Roll:

> **Agility + Ranged Combat**

or:

> **Agility + Heavy Weapons**

depending on weapon.

One uncancelled success hits.

Extra successes may improve the result.

## 7.12 Close Combat

Roll:

> **Strength + Close Combat**

The defender may Block or Dodge.

One remaining success hits.

## 7.13 Extra Successes

After achieving the success needed to hit, spend extra successes on:

- +1 Damage per success;
- +1 Suppression per success;
- knock target Prone;
- force target from Cover;
- create a positional advantage;
- activate a weapon trait if required.

The GM must approve narrative effects.

## 7.14 Called Shots

A Called Shot requires **2 successes** to hit.

Examples:

- exposed sensor organ;
- weapon arm;
- weak armor plate;
- Tanker underside;
- control mechanism.

If successful, apply the specific effect established by the target or GM.

- Bulk
- Traits

| ROF | Available Modes |

| 1 | Single |

## 8.2 Core Weapons

| Weapon | Damage | Range | ROF | Ammo | Bulk | Traits |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| Service Pistol | 1 | Medium | 1 | d8 | 1 | Sidearm |
| Service Rifle | 2 | Long | 3 | d10 | 2 | Burst |
| Carbine | 2 | Medium | 3 | d10 | 2 | Compact |

| Sniper Rifle | 3 | Extreme | 1 | d8 | 3 | Accurate |
| Shotgun | 3 | Close | 1 | d8 | 2 | Scatter, Limited |
| Grenade Launcher | 4 | Medium | 1 | d6 | 2 | Blast, Limited |
| Rocket Launcher | 6 | Long | 1 | Single | 4 | Anti-Armor, Blast, Heavy |

## 8.3 Firing Modes

### Single

No attack bonus.

Ammo depletes on an Ammo Die result of:

> **1**

### Burst

Gain:

> **1 Bonus Die**

Ammo depletes on:

> **1-2**

### Full Auto

Gain:

> **2 Bonus Dice**

Ammo depletes on:

> **1-3**

Full Auto may attack multiple targets in the same Zone.

Assign at least one success to each target hit.

### Suppressive Fire

Suppressive Fire targets a Zone rather than one individual.

Roll the appropriate attack Skill.

On at least one success:

- exposed human or similar targets in the Zone gain 1 Suppression;
- every 2 additional successes may add 1 more Suppression to one target;
- a Horde loses 1 Momentum.

With 3 or more total successes, a Horde also loses 1 Strength.

Ammo depletes on:

> **1-3**

## 8.4 Ammo Checks

Every firearm attack includes an Ammo Check unless the weapon has special ammunition rules.

Roll the weapon's current Ammo Die.

If the result falls within the depletion range for the firing mode, step the Ammo Die down:

> d12 -> d10 -> d8 -> d6 -> Empty

When Empty, the weapon cannot fire.

### Example

A Service Rifle is at d10 Ammo.

The trooper fires Burst.

The attack gains 1 Bonus Die.

The Ammo Die rolls 2.

Because Burst depletes on 1-2:

> d10 becomes d8.

## 8.5 Reloading

Reloading a standard weapon is a Fast Action.

Spend one carried reload and restore the weapon to its listed Ammo Die.

A Heavy or awkward weapon may require a Slow Action.

## 8.6 Limited

A Limited weapon increases its depletion range by 1.

Examples:

A Shotgun firing Single depletes on:

> 1-2

A Grenade Launcher firing Single depletes on:

> 1-2

## 8.7 Single-Use Weapons

A Rocket Launcher becomes Empty after firing.

Reloading requires:

- a carried rocket;
- a Slow Action.

## 8.8 Weapon Traits

### Accurate

When Aiming, gain **2 Bonus Dice** instead of 1.

### Anti-Armor

Reduce target Armor by **3** before Armor Dice are rolled.

### Blast

On a successful attack, the primary target and up to two additional exposed targets in the same Zone suffer the attack.

Against a Horde, increase Horde Strength loss by **1**.

### Burst

The weapon supports Burst fire.

### Compact

Ignore the first penalty for cramped spaces or firing from restricted positions.

### Heavy

If not Braced or properly supported, step the attack Skill Die down once.

### Scatter

At Close range, gain 1 Bonus Die.

At Medium range, step the attack Skill Die down once.

The weapon cannot normally attack beyond Medium.

### Sidearm

May be fired while Engaged without the normal close-quarters penalty.

### Suppressive

Gain 1 Bonus Die when using Suppressive Fire.

# CHAPTER 9
# CASUALTIES

## 9.1 Damage

A successful attack inflicts:

> **Weapon Damage + purchased extra Damage**

Damage is reduced by Armor.

Remaining Damage reduces Health.

## 9.2 Armor

Armor is rated in **Armor Dice**.

Roll a number of d6 equal to the effective Armor rating.

Each **6** cancels 1 Damage.

### Example

A trooper in Armor 3 is hit for 3 Damage.

The player rolls:

> 3d6

and scores one 6.

Armor cancels 1 Damage.

The trooper loses:

> 2 Health.

## 9.3 Armor Types

| Armor | Rating | Bulk | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Standard Combat Armor | 3 | 2 | Helmet, comms, field protection |
| Advanced Combat Armor | 4 | 2 | Better plating and integrated systems |
| Powered Armor Prototype | 6 | — | Optional advanced campaign equipment |

Powered Armor requires additional rules and is not assumed in the standard campaign.

## 9.4 Armor-Piercing Effects

Traits such as Anti-Armor reduce Armor before rolling.

Armor can never be reduced below 0.

## 9.5 Broken

When Health reaches 0, the character is **Broken**.

Immediately:

1. roll a Critical Injury;
2. fall Prone unless impossible;
3. the character cannot act normally.

A Broken character may attempt one action by making a:

> **Composure Test**

See Suppression and Resolve.

On success, take either one Slow Action or one Fast Action.

After doing so, gain 1 Suppression.

## 9.6 Taking Damage While Broken

If a Broken character suffers additional Damage:

- roll another Critical Injury;
- apply both injuries;
- fatal results become increasingly likely.

The GM may rule overwhelming damage kills instantly.

## 9.7 First Aid

A character may attempt:

> **Wits + Medical**

on a Broken character.

With 1 success:

- stabilize the casualty;
- restore 1 Health;
- stop one active fatal timer if the injury permits treatment.

A character can normally benefit from this Health restoration only once per incident.

## 9.8 Critical Injuries

Roll 1d12.

| d12 | Injury | Effect | Treatment |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concussion | Wits stepped down once | 1 success |
| 2 | Broken Arm | Cannot use two-handed weapon normally | 1 success |
| 3 | Broken Leg | Cannot Sprint; movement impaired | 1 success |
| 4 | Crushed Hand | Drop held item; hand unusable | 2 successes |
| 5 | Severe Burn | All physical tests stepped down once | 2 successes |
| 6 | Punctured Lung | Athletics and Mobility stepped down once | 2 successes |
| 7 | Internal Trauma | Lose 1 Health after strenuous scene until treated | 2 successes |
| 8 | Severe Bleeding | Fatal in 1d6 rounds if untreated | 2 successes |
| 9 | Eye Injury | Recon and ranged attacks stepped down once | 2 successes |
| 10 | Severed Limb | Fatal in 1d6 rounds; limb lost | 3 successes |
| 11 | Massive Trauma | Fatal in 1d6 rounds | 3 successes |
| 12 | Mortal Wound | Fatal in 1d6 rounds; treatment is Severe | 3 successes |

The GM may reinterpret an injury to fit the attack.

## 9.9 Recovery

### Short Rest

After 10 minutes in relative safety:

- remove 1 Suppression;
- reload and reorganize equipment if ammunition is available.

### Full Rest

After at least 6 hours of safe rest:

- restore 2 Health;
- reduce Suppression to 0.

Critical Injuries may limit healing.

### Medical Care

A successful Medical treatment during a Full Rest restores:

> +1 additional Health.

Serious injuries may require days, surgery, prosthetics, or evacuation.

# CHAPTER 10
# KEEP IT TOGETHER

A soldier does not need to be wounded to become combat ineffective.

**Suppression** measures:

- fear;
- shock;
- disorientation;
- exhaustion under fire;
- battlefield overload.

## 10.1 Gaining Suppression

Common sources:

- explosions;
- friendly casualties;
- sudden Bug charges;
- being surrounded;
- heavy incoming fire;
- Plasma bombardment;
- terrifying organisms;
- pushing a roll;
- failed morale effects.

## 10.2 Suppression States

| Suppression | State |
|---:|---|
| 0-1 | Effective |
| 2 | Shaken |
| 3 | Suppressed |
| 4+ | Pinned |

In addition, if Suppression reaches or exceeds **Resolve**, make a Panic Test.

## 10.3 Shaken

At Suppression 2:

- step Recon down once;
- step Command down once.

## 10.4 Suppressed

At Suppression 3:

- apply Shaken effects;
- step combat Skill Dice down once;
- advancing toward the source of Suppression requires a Composure Test.

## 10.5 Pinned

At Suppression 4+:

- apply Suppressed effects;
- character must seek or remain in Cover if possible;
- character cannot voluntarily advance toward the main threat without a successful Composure Test;
- Full Auto cannot be used offensively unless the character succeeds at a Composure Test.

## 10.6 Composure Test

A Composure Test is:

> **Empathy + Command**

If Command is 0, roll Empathy only.

Composure is used for:

- resisting Panic;
- acting while Broken;
- advancing while heavily Suppressed;
- maintaining discipline in extreme situations.

## 10.7 Panic Test

When Suppression reaches or exceeds Resolve:

> Make a Composure Test.

On success:

- no Panic result occurs;
- current Suppression remains.

On failure:

- roll on the Panic Table;
- after resolving the result, reduce Suppression to **Resolve - 1**.

## 10.8 Panic Table

Roll 1d6.

| d6 | Result |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Freeze:** Lose the next Slow Action. |
| 2 | **Dive for Cover:** Immediately move to nearest available Cover and go Prone. |
| 3 | **Fall Back:** Move one Zone away from the primary threat. |
| 4 | **Wild Fire:** If holding a loaded firearm, make an immediate unaimed attack at the nearest visible threat; Ammo automatically steps down once afterward. |
| 5 | **Disoriented:** Gain no Fast Action on the next turn. |
| 6 | **Break:** Spend the next turn retreating toward safety, command, or extraction. |

If a result makes no sense, the GM selects the closest appropriate result.

## 10.9 Rallying

A Rally action uses:

> **Empathy + Command**

Default Rally is a Slow Action.

One success removes:

> **1 Suppression**

from one ally in the same or adjacent Zone who can hear the character.

Each extra success may:

- remove another Suppression from the same target;
- remove 1 Suppression from another eligible target.

A character may Rally themselves only if they have a Specialty or no ally is available, at GM discretion.

## 10.10 Bugs and Suppression

Most Bugs have the **Fearless** trait.

Fearless Bugs:

- ignore normal Suppression;
- do not Panic.

Bug Hordes instead use **Momentum**.

# CHAPTER 11
# LEAD THE SQUAD

Command actions turn a collection of soldiers into a unit.

Unless a rule says otherwise, Command actions use:

> **Empathy + Command**

## 11.1 Rally

See Suppression.

## 11.2 Focus Fire

**Slow Action**

Choose one visible target.

On success, up to three allies who can hear the leader gain:

> **1 Bonus Die**

on their next attack against that target before the leader's next turn.

Extra successes may add one additional ally per success.

## 11.3 Fall Back

**Slow Action**

Choose allies or one NPC Squad in the same or adjacent Zone.

On success, affected targets may move one Zone away from the threat without suffering immediate pursuit or reaction attacks.

If they reach Solid or Fortified Cover, remove 1 Suppression from one affected target.

## 11.4 Hold Position

**Slow Action**

On success, affected allies in the leader's Zone gain:

> **1 Bonus Die on Composure Tests**

until the leader's next turn.

They lose the bonus if they voluntarily retreat.

## 11.5 Covering Fire

**Slow Action**

Make Suppressive Fire against a Zone.

Until the attacker's next turn, enemies firing on allied characters moving through the suppressed Zone step their attack Skill Die down once.

## 11.6 Fire at Will

**Slow Action**

Choose one ally or NPC Squad.

On success:

- target gains 1 Bonus Die on its next attack;
- if the target is a PC using a firearm, its next Ammo Check depletion range increases by 1.

Fire at Will trades ammunition for immediate violence.

## 11.7 Move! Move! Move!

**Slow Action**

On success, one ally or NPC Squad that can hear the leader may immediately move one Zone.

A PC who uses this movement spends their next available Fast Action.

## 11.8 Reorganize

Normally used outside Engaged combat.

Two depleted friendly NPC Squads may be combined.

The new Squad:

- uses the better Firepower rating;
- uses the better Morale rating;
- combines current Strength;
- cannot exceed the sum of surviving personnel.

# CHAPTER 12
# WHEN THEY COME IN THOUSANDS

A Horde represents a mass of similar enemies.

Do not place or track every creature.

A Horde uses:

- Strength
- Armor
- Attack Die
- Damage
- Suppression
- Speed
- Momentum
- Traits

## 12.1 Horde Strength

| Strength | Approximate Number |
|---:|---|
| 2 | 3-5 |
| 4 | 6-10 |
| 6 | 11-20 |
| 8 | 21-40 |
| 10 | 40-80 |
| 12+ | Massive wave |

These numbers are descriptive.

## 12.2 Horde Attacks

A Horde rolls:

> **Attack Die + Strength Bonus Dice**

| Current Strength | Bonus Dice |
|---:|---:|
| 1-3 | 0 |
| 4-6 | 1 |
| 7-9 | 2 |
| 10+ | 3 |

One success hits.

Extra successes may increase Damage or affect additional targets when the fiction permits.

## 12.3 Damaging a Horde

After Armor and other defenses, convert Damage into Strength loss.

| Damage | Horde Strength Loss |
|---:|---:|
| 1-2 | 1 |
| 3-4 | 2 |
| 5+ | 3 |

**Blast** increases Strength loss by 1.

A Horde at Strength 0 is:

- destroyed;
- dispersed;
- routed;
- no longer combat effective.

## 12.4 Momentum

Momentum represents whether a Horde is stalled or overrunning the defenders.

| Momentum | State |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Stalled |
| 1 | Pressing |
| 2 | Charging |
| 3 | Overrunning |

Momentum cannot exceed 3 or fall below 0.

### Gain Momentum

Typical triggers:

- Horde advances successfully under fire;
- Horde wins a mass melee;
- defenders abandon a Zone;
- command organism directs the swarm.

### Lose Momentum

Typical triggers:

- Suppressive Fire succeeds;
- Blast attack inflicts Strength loss;
- major obstacle stops advance;
- leader organism is killed.

## 12.5 Overrun

At Momentum 3, a Horde may:

- enter defended Zones more easily;
- ignore one movement obstruction;
- make an immediate melee attack after breaching a position if the GM judges it appropriate.

## 12.6 Horde Movement

A Horde normally moves a number of Zones equal to Speed.

Most Warrior Hordes have:

> **Speed 1**

Fast swarms may have more.

## 12.7 Hordes in Melee

When a Horde occupies the same Zone as defenders:

- the defenders are Engaged;
- leaving normally requires Mobility or a successful Fall Back order;
- the Horde may spread attacks among multiple targets.

The GM should describe individual Bugs without creating individual turns.

What must be accomplished?

What would improve the strategic outcome?

How does the squad enter?

## 15.2 Escalation Clock

Use a six-step clock.

| Level | State |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Quiet |
| 1 | Contact |
| 2 | Reinforcements |
| 3 | Swarm Pressure |
| 4 | Heavy Organism |
| 5 | Position Overrun |
| 6 | Catastrophic Escalation |

Advance Escalation when:

- the squad delays;
- loud actions attract Bugs;
- an objective fails;
- a scripted event occurs;
- the GM needs to increase pressure.

The clock is not required to advance every round.

It measures the overall deterioration of the mission.

Common requirements:

- hold an LZ for a number of rounds;
- activate a beacon;
- restore communications;
- reach a tunnel exit;
- recover a vehicle;
- survive until dropship arrival.

Do not assume victory ends combat.

Sometimes the hardest part of a mission is getting out.

# CHAPTER 17
# RUNNING THE WAR

## 17.1 Keep the Objective Visible

Players should usually understand what they are trying to accomplish even when they do not know the full situation.

Good objectives:

- recover the black box;
- hold the transmitter for six rounds;
- destroy the nest entrance;
- escort the engineer;
- locate survivors;
- mark the Plasma Bug;
- reach extraction.

"Kill everything" should be rare.

## 17.2 Use Bugs as Battlefield Problems

A Bug should change what the players must do.

Examples:

**Warriors**
- close distance and overwhelm.

**Hoppers**
- bypass walls and hit support personnel.

**Tankers**
- break fortified positions.

**Plasma Bugs**
- make staying in one place dangerous.

**Brain Bugs**
- strengthen the swarm and become strategic targets.

## 17.3 Use Hordes Early

If there are more enemies than the GM wants to track individually:

> use a Horde.

The game is designed around that choice.

## 17.4 Separate Threat from Body Count

A Strength 8 Horde might represent:

- 25 large Bugs;
- 40 smaller Bugs;
- 70 immature attackers.

Strength measures battlefield effectiveness, not exact population.

## 17.5 Starting Encounter Guidelines

For four starting PCs:

### Light

- 4-6 individual Warriors;
or
- one Strength 4 Horde.

### Standard

- one Strength 6 Horde;
or
- 4 Warriors plus one Hopper.

### Hard

- one Strength 8 Horde plus a specialist Bug;
or
- two Strength 4 Hordes.

### Severe

- two Strength 6 Hordes;
or
- one Tanker with supporting Warriors.

These are playtest guidelines, not fixed balance formulas.

## 17.6 Keep Ammunition Relevant

Ammunition should influence decisions without becoming accounting.

Use:

- long defensive fights;
- uncertain resupply;
- Full Auto temptation;
- mission loadout choices.

Do not arbitrarily remove ammunition just to punish players.

## 17.7 Apply Suppression Fictionally

Suppression is not only gunfire.

Examples:

- a squadmate is torn apart;
- a Tanker breaches the wall;
- a tunnel collapses;
- the dropship is destroyed;
- Bugs emerge behind the squad.

If the event would make trained soldiers lose focus, Suppression may be appropriate.

## 17.8 Casualties Should Change the Battlefield

When friendly NPC Squads lose Strength:

- reduce firepower;
- create wounded;
- open defensive gaps;
- force players into leadership roles;
- threaten mission objectives.

Casualties should matter beyond a number.

## 17.9 Failure Should Escalate

A failed roll should not always mean "nothing happens."

Good consequences:

- lose time;
- gain Suppression;
- Ammo depletes;
- attract Bugs;
- damage equipment;
- increase Escalation;
- expose a position;
- complete the task at a cost.

## 17.10 Major Monsters

A Monster should usually have:

- one obvious strength;
- one exploitable weakness;
- one battlefield-changing action.

Do not make a Monster simply a large pile of Health.

# CHAPTER 20
# DESIGNER’S PLAYTEST NOTES

BUG Wars is intended to be tested before rules expansion.

The first major milestone is not a large equipment catalog or vehicle chapter.

It is this encounter:

> Four to six troopers and a small friendly unit defend an isolated position against approximately one hundred Bugs while ammunition declines, Suppression rises, the line takes casualties, a heavy organism breaches the perimeter, and extraction becomes the final objective.

The core system is working when:

- the GM does not track individual swarm creatures;
- a Horde turn takes less than one minute;
- players voluntarily use Suppressive Fire;
- leaders voluntarily use Command actions;
- ammunition changes player decisions;
- Heavy Weapons have a distinct role;
- Suppression changes tactics without removing players from the game too often;
- Critical Injuries create urgency;
- a large battle takes about as long as a normal medium-sized RPG combat.

## 20.1 Recommended Test Sequence

### Test A — Fire Team

4 PCs vs. 8 individual Warriors.

Test:

- core attacks;
- Armor;
- Damage;
- reactions;
- Critical Injuries.

### Test B — Horde

4 PCs plus one friendly Squad vs. a Strength 8 Warrior Horde.

Test:

- Horde Damage;
- Momentum;
- Suppressive Fire;
- NPC Squad rules.

### Test C — Tanker

4-6 PCs vs. one Tanker with limited support.

Test:

- Anti-Armor;
- Called Shots;
- heavy weapons;
- Monster pacing.

### Test D — Outpost

Run **Hold the Line**.

Test everything together.

## 20.2 Record These Metrics

During testing, record:

- average rounds per encounter;
- average attacks before a weapon reaches Empty;
- PC Health lost;
- number of Critical Injuries;
- Suppression gained;
- Rally actions used;
- Horde turns per battle;
- Horde survival time;
- NPC Squad losses;
- Tanker survival time;
- total session combat time.

Change rules from evidence rather than intuition.

# APPENDIX D
# THE LOOK OF BUG WARS

This section is not rules text. It demonstrates a possible visual direction for a finished publication.

## Visual Identity

BUG Wars should feel like a **military field manual that has survived an alien war**.

Useful visual motifs include:

- worn deployment folders;
- stenciled military typography;
- unit patches;
- tactical overlays;
- reconnaissance photography;
- biological sketches and autopsy diagrams;
- warning markings used sparingly;
- intelligence briefings;
- casualty cards;
- mission stamps;
- handwritten field notes.

Avoid reproducing the visual identity of existing military science-fiction franchises. BUG Wars should become identifiable at a glance as its own game.

## Suggested Color Direction

A distinctive palette could use:

- deep charcoal;
- bone white;
- oxidized orange;
- muted cyan instrumentation;
- mineral green for Bug biology.

## Human Equipment

Infantry equipment should feel mass-produced, modular, field-repairable, and military rather than superheroic. Armor should show scratches, unit markings, ammunition pouches, mission equipment, and field repairs.

## Bug Design

Avoid making every organism simply “a different insect.” Treat the enemy as a biological military ecosystem with recognizable shared anatomy but radically different battlefield functions:

- assault organisms;
- tunneling organisms;
- command organisms;
- artillery organisms;
- aerial organisms;
- siege organisms.

## Interior Illustration Plan

A 150-200 page finished edition could use roughly:

- 8-12 full-page chapter illustrations;
- 20-30 half-page combat or setting illustrations;
- 30+ small Bug and equipment spot illustrations;
- tactical diagrams;
- archetype portraits;
- weapon silhouettes;
- mission maps.

# APPENDIX E
# SAMPLE PAGE TREATMENTS

## Player Rule Page

**Top margin:** Chapter title and field-manual classification.

**Main column:** Core rule.

**Outer sidebar:** Example, warning, or tactical advice.

**Bottom strip:** One-line rule summary.

> **SUPPRESSION**
>
> You do not need to be wounded to stop functioning. Explosions, casualties, charging Bugs, and collapsing positions all build Suppression.
>
> **FIELD RULE:** At Suppression equal to Resolve, make a Panic Test.

Each Bug entry could contain:

- large silhouette or illustration;
- battlefield role;
- statistics;
- recognizable behavior;
- Trooper Advice sidebar;
